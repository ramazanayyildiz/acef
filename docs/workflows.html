Match process to work

More risk should mean more confidence—not repeated ceremony.

ACEF chooses two things separately: how much delivery structure the work needs, and how much checking its risk requires.

The decision model

Scope chooses the workflow. Risk chooses the safeguards.

1 · Delivery depth

Is this a contained project-native task, a focused defect, a standard change, or planning-heavy work?

+
2 · Assurance level

Are normal checks enough, or does a high-risk boundary require extra safeguards?

Guarded is not another workflow. It strengthens the selected workflow. It must not repeat planning, implementation, review, and closeout a second time.
Decision 1

Choose the delivery depth

Project’s normal workflow

Contained, reversible work with one technical boundary and one product surface. No ACEF run artifacts.

Focused fix (ACEF Fix)

A proven, bounded defect: reproduce, patch, focused test, one independent review, close.

Standard delivery (ACEF Standard)

A scoped ordinary change that benefits from a short plan, implementation, verification, and review.

Planned delivery (ACEF Full)

One coherent, broad or ambiguous contract that genuinely needs detailed planning and independent lifecycle roles.

What qualifies for the project’s normal workflow?

The work must be reversible, contained, and limited to one technical boundary and one product surface. Examples include a copy correction, a local layout adjustment, or a narrow bug with no security, persistence, provider, realtime, state-machine, analytics, or coordination trigger.

Focused tests are still required. “No ACEF run” does not mean “no verification.”

Why ACEF Full is not a container for many small defects

Several unrelated root causes do not become one coherent story merely because they were found in the same audit or CI run. ACEF splits them first, then admits each child independently. Many children will return to the project’s normal workflow or ACEF Fix.

Decision 2

Add safeguards only when the boundary needs them

Normal checks (Baseline)

The evidence and independent review appropriate to the chosen workflow.

Extra safeguards (Guarded)

Stronger scope control, boundary-focused evidence, and explicit approval where needed. It adds confidence, not a duplicate lifecycle.

Extra safeguards normally apply to security, privacy, permissions, payments, migrations, provider integrations, realtime behavior, concurrency, state machines, analytics, irreversible effects, and other shared safety boundaries.

Can a small patch still need extra safeguards?

Yes. Patch size and risk are different. A thirteen-line authorization or billing-state fix may use ACEF Fix with Guarded assurance: a compact lifecycle with stronger evidence. High risk does not automatically require ACEF Full.

Built-in limit

ACEF stops runaway review loops

Renaming a run does not make it new work. ACEF tracks repeated attempts under the same underlying goal. When reviews and replans keep growing without product progress, it stops opening fresh process cycles and asks the team to consolidate the known problems or split the scope.

Consolidate

Finish manual QA, gather non-critical findings into one shared list, and repair related items together where safe.

Stop and split

If the same objective keeps reopening or replanning, suspend it and require a human-approved scope split.

Exact counters and state names

ACEF enters consolidation at five runs, three replans, six review cycles, or 30 active control minutes without a product/test commit. It refuses an attempted eighth run or a fifth replan. Exact status constants, defect classes, and worktree freshness behavior are in the technical reference.
